# Mortgage-centered fixed income atlas

The September 19, 2026 expansion keeps Mortgage Map as the public identity and adds three overlapping views of the same catalog: Mortgage, Rates and All fixed income. Stable concept IDs, public sources, typed relationships and one reading history connect the views. Global search, comparisons and connection studies remain available from every lens.

This iteration adds 36 concepts, 65 explicitly authored relationships and six guided paths. Public-source checks include FHFA credit-score and loan-limit pages, CFPB lending and disclosure rules, Fannie Mae CLTV/HCLTV guidance, SEC ABS reform and retention releases, CME SOFR and Treasury material, New York Fed term-premium documentation, Cleveland Fed inflation-compensation research, OCC mortgage/CRE handbooks and CREFC servicing definitions. The source registry links the material next to the relevant concepts. Historical rules are identified by adoption date; no current numeric limits or live market quotations are embedded.

## Editorial standard

Each addition should resolve an actual analytical question. Include the mechanism or definition, a distinction that prevents a likely mistake, a check question, related concepts with reasons, and public references. For formulas, state units, conventions and assumptions. Every path needs a premise, a reason for every step and a boundary. Do not imply that classification comparisons are causal arrows. Private reading can identify questions; published explanations must be original and independently supported by public sources.

## Interaction direction

Manual guided steps explain a mechanism while preserving the reader and history. A short reveal marks the active step; reduced-motion users receive static content. Future motion should reveal cash-flow order or a changing dependency using explicitly illustrative states. Avoid autoplay, decorative movement, and implied market forecasts.

Measure usefulness by whether a visitor can explain the distinction, follow the public evidence and move from a market headline to the underlying cash-flow question. Concept count alone does not establish encyclopedia-level depth.
