# Refinancing frictions and prepayment response

Accepted foreground scope, October 21, 2026: use Jingheng’s accessible recent readings to deepen one connected Mortgage Map topic. Follow the foreground engineer and independent reviewer route in `agent_workflow.md`.

## Question and implementation

Why can the same mortgage-rate incentive produce a different repayment pattern, and what does that mean for an MBS model?

The existing `frictions` and `loan_balance` entries now distinguish economic costs, eligibility, attention and execution. Two additional concepts explain monthly principal aggregation and prepayment model stability. The guided path connects these mechanisms to OAS and duration. Existing IDs, full-catalog navigation and history remain intact. All examples are static educational illustrations; there is no forecasting, calibration or pricing tool.

## Public evidence and original synthesis

Sources checked October 21, 2026:

- [Federal Reserve refinancing guide](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/refinancings/default.htm): archived educational explanation of costs, term changes and no-cost financing. Its historical fee ranges and tax examples are not reproduced or treated as current guidance.
- [New York Fed Staff Report 836](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr836.pdf), February 2018, section VI: mortgage technology, refinancing and the authors’ identification caveat. This historical evidence does not estimate the impact of current AI agents.
- [New York Fed Staff Report 45](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr45.html), September 1998: structural change in refinancing behavior, conditional on credit, equity and loan characteristics.
- [New York Fed Staff Report 931](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr931.pdf#page=37), revised October 2021, printed p. 35: SMM denominator and CPR conversion. The weighted aggregation identity follows algebraically from that definition; the dollar examples are original, not empirical estimates.
- [Interagency model risk guidance](https://www.federalreserve.gov/frrs/guidance/supervisory-guidance-on-model-risk-management.htm), April 17, 2026, sections IV–V: temporal testing, outcomes and monitoring. Applying those principles to incentive, balance, age and servicer groups is this map’s educational synthesis, not a prescribed regulatory checklist.
